# GPU confirmation on three GPU types and with the disk cache, 17 November 2026

This file reports the second round of the kit in `gpu/`. The kit ran in full on
a Tesla T4 and on an H100 80GB HBM3, and once more on an A100-SXM4-40GB with
`TRITON_CACHE_AUTOTUNING=1`. All three used torch 2.15.0 and Triton 3.9.0, as
the A100 run of 14 November did, which is the baseline here. Each kernel ran
in its own container and each repeat in a fresh process. The raw files are in
`results/raw/t4/`, `h100/` and `a100-cache/`, and are not tracked.
`python tools/gpu_compare.py` prints every number below.

The A100 count for the minimal demonstration in `results/2026-11-14-gpu.md`
was wrong. A partial config won in 16 of 20 cells, not 17.

## 1. The leak depends on the GPU

| Outcome | T4 | A100 | H100 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Partial winner in the minimal demonstration | 15 of 20 | 16 of 20 | 18 of 20 |
| Kernels with unwritten elements under the poison fill | 15 of 34 | 19 of 34 | 21 of 34 |
| Poison-fill runs with unwritten elements | 68 of 204 | 76 of 204 | 96 of 204 |
| Kernels with the 1-of-5 signature in some run | 13 | 17 | 19 |
| Kernels with unwritten elements whose plain trials all passed | 5 | 7 | 5 |

The totals are close, but they are not the same kernels. Eleven kernels leave
elements unwritten on some GPU types and not on others, in 12 fresh processes
per GPU.

| Kernel | T4 | A100 | H100 |
|---|---|---|---|
| `1586_r2` | 0 of 6 | 6 of 6 | 5 of 6 |
| `2121_r2` | 0 of 6 | 6 of 6 | 4 of 6 |
| `6897_r4` | 0 of 6 | 6 of 6 | 5 of 6 |
| `5338_r2` | 0 of 6 | 2 of 6 | 6 of 6 |
| `984_r2` | 0 of 6 | 2 of 6 | 5 of 6 |
| `703_r1` | 0 of 6 | 1 of 6 | 0 of 6 |
| `8853_r2` | 0 of 6 | 0 of 6 | 5 of 6 |
| `1797_r3` | 0 of 6 | 0 of 6 | 3 of 6 |

| `3828_r2` | 5 of 6 | 0 of 6 | 0 of 6 |
| `7485_r3` | 6 of 6 | 0 of 6 | 0 of 6 |

Each cell counts the poison-fill runs with unwritten elements. The first nine
rows never leave an element unwritten on the T4, and their plain trials all
pass there. On the A100 or the H100 they do leave elements unwritten. For
`2121_r2` every plain trial fails on the A100, and for `5338_r2` and `8853_r2`
every plain trial fails on the H100. The code and inputs are the same, so the
difference is which config wins the timing race. On the T4, eight of the nine
only ever chose a config between 2 and 4. On the A100 or the H100, config 0
won for all nine. A kernel that passes every check on a T4, a common CI GPU,
can return partly uninitialized output on an A100 or H100.

`1797_r3`, `6993_r1` and `8853_r2` were among the nine kernels marked "not
confirmed" on 14 November. They are now confirmed on the H100.

**Out-of-bounds reads.** `7350_r1` raised an illegal memory access in every run
on the A100 and on the H100. On the T4 every run completed and every plain
trial passed. The same out-of-bounds read faults on two GPU types and goes
unnoticed on the third.

**Four kernels that crashed in KernelGYM.** `236_r3`, `481_r3`, `3725_r4` and
`6133_r2` ran clean with `CUDA_LAUNCH_BLOCKING=1` on all three GPUs. On the
H100, `6133_r2` fails every trial in both modes, with no unwritten element, no
NaN and no marker value. This is a numerical mismatch, not a memory error, and
it has not been read yet.

## 2. The disk cache removes the covered first call

With `TRITON_CACHE_AUTOTUNING=1` the autotuner writes its timings to the Triton
cache after tuning. A later process with the same kernel, key and configs
reads that file and launches only the cached winner, without benchmarking any
config (`triton/runtime/autotuner.py`, `check_disk_cache`). All processes in a
container shared one cache directory. The first process that launched a kernel
tuned it.

**Minimal demonstration.** Process 0 tuned, and its first call left 0 elements
unwritten, as without the cache. In processes 1 to 4 the first call left
786,432 elements unwritten at n = 1,048,576 and 12,582,912 at n = 16,777,216,
where block 256 won. At the two smaller sizes a covering block won and nothing
was left unwritten.

**The flagged kernels.** 273 later processes launched an autotuned kernel, and
all 273 loaded the cached result: their first call ran no benchmark and their
timings equal the tuning process's. Four kernels, `228_r2`,
`1918_r2`, `4163_r2` and `4270_r2`, launch only in eval mode, so their first
eval process tuned. In 58 poison-fill runs the first call left elements
unwritten.

| Plain runs on the A100 | Without the cache | With the cache |
|---|---|---|
| All five trials pass | 152 | 144 |
| Only the first trial passes | 46 | 0 |
| No trial passes | 6 | 60 |

The 1-of-5 signature disappears. Without the cache, the first call is always
covered by tuning. With the cache, only the first process on a machine tunes,
and every later process gets a partial write from its first call on.

Two consequences follow. A benchmark that uses the cache sees the defect in all
five trials, so it rejects more of these kernels. A deployment that uses the
cache loses the covered first call, so after a restart the first request
already returns uninitialized memory. The cache also fixes the winner per
machine, so the variation between processes reported on 14 November turns into
variation between machines.

Seven of the 34 kernels have no autotuning and are not part of this count.
`1586_r2`, `5239_r2` and `7962_r1` leave elements unwritten from the first call
and still pass every plain trial, because of stale allocator memory.
